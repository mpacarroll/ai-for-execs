# Skill 1: pick the first task

**The rule:** start where being wrong is cheap and being right is visible. Not where the
value is.

## Why

The first task's job is not to produce value. It is to earn the right to do the second
task. You are buying evidence and institutional confidence, and you should pay for them
with the cheapest thing you own.

## The two axes

- **Cost of being wrong.** Drafting an internal summary somebody reads anyway: cheap.
  Anything touching a customer record, a payment, a filing, or a safety decision: not.
- **Frequency.** A task that happens fifty times a day produces enough evidence to judge
  within a fortnight. A quarterly task tells you nothing for a year.

You want high frequency and low cost of error. That corner exists in every organization,
and it is almost never the task the sponsor first suggests.

## The test

Ask: if this goes badly, can we say so out loud in a meeting without anyone's job being
at risk? If the answer is no, it is not a first task. Pick again.

## The common mistake

Starting with the highest-value process, because that is where the money is. Highest
value means highest cost of being wrong, and at the start you have no evidence that
anything works. Value is what the third task is for.
