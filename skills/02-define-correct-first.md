# Skill 2: define correct before building

**The rule:** three questions, in writing, agreed by the process owner, before anything
is built. If the second one cannot be answered, stop.

## The three questions

1. **What must this produce?** Usually easy. Usually the only one that gets asked.
2. **How will we know it is right?** The one that carries everything. Almost everyone
   skips it, because it feels obvious until you try to write it down.
3. **What must it never do?** The one that prevents the expensive failure class.

## Why the second question is the whole game

Most stalled adoption programs stalled here: nobody could say what a correct answer
looked like, so nobody could tell whether the system worked, so nobody would approve it
touching a real decision. In a regulated setting, an unwritten standard is
indistinguishable from no standard, and a reviewer with no standard correctly declines.

The written definition of correct is not paperwork. It is the artifact that unblocks
approval.

## Why the third question earns its place

One-line rules like "never invent a number" and "never guess at a date" eliminate whole
categories of failure. They are almost never written down because they feel too obvious
to state. They are not obvious to a system that would otherwise fill the gap with a
plausible guess.

## If you cannot answer question two

The task is not ready, and discovering that in a meeting costs an hour. Discovering it
after two months of building costs the two months and some of the program's
credibility. Treat the unanswerable task as your most useful early finding, park it, and
pick one you can specify.
