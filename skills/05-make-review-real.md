# Skill 5: make the human step real

**The rule:** "a human reviews the output" is the most common control and the most
commonly fictional one. Design for the reviewer's real behavior, not their described
behavior.

## Why the control goes fictional

A reviewer facing forty plausible-looking items with nothing marking which are doubtful
approves all forty by Friday. Not because they are lazy, but because the system gave
them no way to spend attention where it matters. The control still exists on the process
diagram. It stopped existing in the world.

## What makes it real

The system has to be uncertain out loud:

- Surface what it was unsure about, and why.
- Show its source next to its conclusion, so checking is one glance rather than a search.
- Make every assumption visible. If the system had to assume something to proceed, that
  belongs in a field the reviewer can scan, not buried in logic. Nobody can scan for an
  assumption that left no trace.
- Route genuinely ambiguous cases to a queue rather than filing them with a guess. A
  wrong value in a record is worse than a gap in a queue.

## The test

Watch a real reviewer for twenty minutes. If they are approving items faster than they
could possibly be checking them, the control is fictional, and the fix is in the system's
design, not in telling the reviewer to be more careful.
