# Skill 7: what to refuse

**The rule:** a sequence is defined as much by what it declines as what it does. These
five refusals are load-bearing.

## Refuse to start with the highest-value process

Highest value means highest cost of error, and at the start you have no evidence. The
first task buys evidence cheaply (skill 1). Value comes later, and faster, because of it.

## Refuse to begin with a platform decision

Choosing tooling before one task works is committing before learning. The platform
decision is cheap to revisit after three working tasks and expensive to make before one.

## Refuse the maturity model

Maturity models describe organizations that already succeeded, in the order it looked
like afterwards. They are retrospectives sold as plans. Your sequence should be driven
by your evidence, not by a diagram of someone else's.

## Refuse the proof of concept with no definition of success

It will succeed, because an undefined bar is always cleared, and it will teach nothing.
Every experiment gets a written pass condition before it runs, or it does not run.

## Refuse to let enthusiasm set the sequence

The loudest internal advocate is a genuine asset and a poor prioritization function.
Their energy is fuel; the sequence comes from skills 1 and 2, which do not care who is
excited.
